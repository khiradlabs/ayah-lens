## opening

For many who don’t understand Arabic, the sound is familiar. Two billion Muslims, roughly a quarter of humanity. The potential impact is enormous: helping listeners follow the meaning of a daily recitation, without interrupting it. We want to understand what Allah is saying. But stopping to look up the meaning takes us away from the recitation. What if your glasses became your personal Quran translator? The meaning, right in front of your eyes. The text follows the recitation automatically. No scrolling. No tapping. Just listen. And understand.

## product

Meet Ayah Lens: Meta Ray-Ban Display, Muse Voice and Muse Spark, bringing Quran recitation and its published English meaning into view.

## use_cases

The Quran is part of five daily prayers. Imagine following the meaning during prayer, or listening to a reciter on television, with your hands free.

## meta_challenge

The Quran contains over six thousand verses. Finding the right one from live recitation is challenging. Phrases repeat across different passages, and a listener can join halfway through a verse.

## meta_pipeline

The glasses capture the recitation. Muse Voice turns that audio into a live Arabic transcript. Our software searches the Quran reference and tracks the listener’s position, even inside a long verse. Muse Spark helps resolve uncertain matches and pair Arabic portions with published English meaning. Readable portions reach the glasses display automatically as the recitation continues. Our software checks that the displayed wording comes directly from the published sources. Spark’s pairing plans are cached for these demonstrations. Transcription and passage tracking run live.

## hifz

For Hifz, or Quran memorization, you can turn off English and keep Arabic in view while rehearsing. Turn the meaning back on whenever you want to follow it.

## foundation

Built during this hackathon, the prototype covers the complete Quran reference. Fifty-nine core tests check matching, source preservation and display delivery.
